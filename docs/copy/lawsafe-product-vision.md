## **LawSafe**: Lawyer as a Service for Every Indian

_Project name:_ **_LawSafe_**_. Internal workshop / beta codename:_ **_NyayaSetu_**_._

_A founder's vision document for the team and our earliest stakeholders. This document describes only what we are building and why — the destination, not the route._

## TL;DR — The Vision in Three Lines

- **We are building India's first AI-native "Lawyer as a Service" platform** — a chat-first mobile app that lets any citizen describe a legal problem in plain language and instantly get a grounded, cited explanation of their rights and options, then connects them to a verified, specializing advocate when human counsel is needed.
- **Our purpose is to close the access-to-justice gap** in a country where the National Judicial Data Grid records over 4.7 crore cases pending in subordinate courts, more than 63 lakh in the High Courts, and a Supreme Court backlog at an all-time high of 88,417 cases (Vision IAS/The Hindu, September 2025) — and where DAKSH's Access to Justice Survey led co-founder Harish Narasappa to conclude that around 60 per cent of Indians do not have access to justice. We make first-mile legal understanding free, instant, vernacular, and trustworthy. Available in all local Indian languages
- **Our north star is trust, not traffic**: every answer is grounded in current Indian law (Constitution, BNS/BNSS/BSA, civil and criminal codes, and case files), every lawyer is Bar Council-verified, and we operate as a neutral bridge — never a tout — in full compliance with Bar Council of India Rule 36 and the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.

## 0. A Note on the Name

**LawSafe** is the project and product name — the name we build under, ship under, and take to market. It says the promise plainly and in a register every Indian user, in any language, can parse on first contact: _the law, made safe for you._ Safety is not incidental to this product; it is the entire thesis. Safe from ignorance of your own rights. Safe from bad advice. Safe from hallucinated law. Safe from the tout, the fixer, and the unverified "legal influencer." The name is the principle.

**NyayaSetu** — _the bridge to justice_ — is retained as our internal workshop and beta codename. It is what we call the thing while we are still making it: in the repo, in the design files, in the early builds, on the invite lists for our first cohort of citizens and advocates. It carries the civilizational weight of what we are attempting, and it belongs to the people building it.

The two names are not in competition. NyayaSetu is who we are in the workshop; LawSafe is what we hand to the citizen. When the beta ends, the bridge stays — it just gets a name people can spell.

## 1. Vision Statement

**To make understanding your legal rights as easy as sending a message — and acting on them as simple as booking a ride.**

We envision an India where a domestic worker in Patna, a first-time founder in Bengaluru, a widow navigating a succession dispute in rural Odisha, and a salaried employee facing a wrongful termination in Gurugram all begin their legal journey the same way: by opening an app and typing or speaking, in their own language, "Here is what happened to me." Within seconds they receive a clear, accurate, jargon-free explanation of where they stand, what the law says, what their options are, and what it will cost — and, when a human is needed, a shortlist of verified advocates who genuinely specialize in exactly their problem.

We are not building a chatbot with a lawyer directory bolted on. We are building the **default first point of contact between an Indian citizen and the legal system.**

## 2. Why We Exist — The Purpose

India does not suffer from a shortage of law. It suffers from a shortage of _access_ to law.

The country has strong statutes, a proud constitutional tradition, and — according to the Ministry of Law & Justice's August 2023 statement to the Rajya Sabha — 20,13,081 registered advocates. And yet justice remains out of reach for most people. The National Judicial Data Grid records over 4.7 crore cases pending in subordinate courts, more than 63 lakh in the High Courts, and a Supreme Court backlog at an all-time high of 88,417 cases; more than 85% of all pending matters languish in the district and subordinate courts. At the current pace, NITI Aayog's 2018 strategy paper famously warned it would take **324 years** to clear the backlog. India has only about **21 judges per million population** — compared with roughly 150 per million in the United States, and against the Law Commission of India's long-standing recommendation of 50 per million. Judicial delay is estimated to cost the economy on the order of **0.5% of GDP** each year (DAKSH/Law Commission Report No. 245, ~₹50,387 crore in 2016 terms), with DAKSH's Harish Narasappa arguing that once litigants' lost wages and out-of-pocket costs are counted the true figure could reach **1.5–2% of GDP**.

But the backlog is only the visible symptom. The deeper problem is that **most Indians never make it to the courthouse door in the first place** — and often shouldn't have to. They don't know their rights. They don't know free legal aid exists: a 2016 Commonwealth Human Rights Initiative survey found nearly **80% of rural Indians were unaware of their right to free legal aid.** They are intimidated by lawyers and by cost, and paralyzed by not knowing whether their problem is even "legal." The result is a vast silent majority who absorb injustice — a cheque that bounced, a deposit a landlord won't return, a fraudulent online transaction, a denied insurance claim, harassment online — because the cost and friction of understanding their options exceeds the wrong itself.

We exist to collapse that friction to near zero. **If a person can describe their problem, they deserve to understand their rights** — immediately, affordably, and in their own words. That is the founding conviction of this company.

This purpose is also well-timed. Per the IAMAI–Kantar _Internet in India Report 2024_, active internet users reached **886 million in 2024** (set to surpass 900 million in 2025), with rural India at 488 million (55% of the total) and **98% of users accessing content in Indic languages** rather than English. The government itself has spent a decade proving demand for exactly this kind of service through the Tele-Law programme, which had **delivered 1 crore pre-litigation advice sessions** and reached 2.1 crore beneficiaries across roughly 2.5 lakh Common Service Centres by early 2025. And after the reforms that took effect on 1 July 2024 — replacing the colonial-era Indian Penal Code, Code of Criminal Procedure, and Indian Evidence Act with the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, and Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam — the entire nation is re-learning its own criminal law simultaneously. The moment for a trustworthy, technology-first legal companion has arrived.

## 3. Who We Serve

**Primary: The ordinary Indian citizen with an ordinary legal problem.** This is the person the legal system currently fails most — not the corporate general counsel with a panel of firms, but the individual who has a genuine legal need and nowhere obvious to turn. They span every income band and every one of India's languages. They are dealing with the everyday matters that make up the substance of real people's legal lives: a rental agreement, a cheque bounce, a traffic dispute, a consumer complaint, a matrimonial matter, a property mutation, a will, a legal heir certificate, an RTI application, an incident of cyber fraud or online harassment.

**Secondary: The small business, founder, and independent professional.** The MSME owner, the D2C founder, the freelancer, the artist — people who need company incorporation, agreements drafted and vetted, IP registered, MCA compliance handled, and debt recovered, but for whom traditional firms are too expensive and too slow. India's standing as one of the world's largest startup ecosystems has created enormous, under-served demand for exactly this "startup legal infrastructure."

**Third, and essential: The lawyer.** We serve advocates as much as citizens. India's legal profession is bimodal — a small elite at the top and a very large number of junior and small-town advocates who struggle to find clients, and to whom the profession's advertising restrictions offer few ethical avenues to be discovered. We serve them by delivering **qualified, context-rich, correctly-matched clients** — a citizen arrives already understanding their problem, with a structured summary in hand — so the lawyer spends time on law, not on triage. For the verified advocate, we are a source of dignified, relevant work.

## 4. What The Product Is

LawSafe is one product experienced through several surfaces, unified by a single promise: **grounded legal understanding, then a trusted human when you need one.**

**The Mobile App — the citizen's front door.** A chat-first experience, deliberately as familiar as Claude or ChatGPT, because that familiarity is the whole point: no forms, no legalese, no intimidation. The user describes their situation in natural language — typed or spoken, in their language — and receives a clear, structured answer: what the law says, which statute or provision applies, what their realistic options are, likely timelines and costs, and the immediate next steps. When the matter needs a human, the app surfaces a curated shortlist of **verified advocates who specialize in precisely that issue**, with transparent credentials. The app is the place where legal anxiety turns into a plan.

**The Legal Intelligence Engine — our domain-trained model.** At the core is a language model built and continuously grounded for Indian law specifically: the Constitution, current statutes, the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita and Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, civil and criminal codes, and a living corpus of case files, affidavits, and standard instruments. It is designed to do what general-purpose chatbots dangerously cannot: reason within the four corners of _current, citable Indian law_ rather than plausible-sounding invention. This is our deepest technical and ethical commitment, and Section 6 explains why we treat it as sacred.

**The Case & Knowledge Store — institutional memory.** A repository of case files, precedents, templates, and reference documents that the engine draws on to ground its answers and that advocates draw on to serve clients faster. Over time this becomes the platform's most valuable asset: a structured, ever-growing, uniquely Indian legal knowledge base.

**The Backend & Records System — the trust and matching layer.** The systems that verify every advocate's Bar Council credentials, match citizens to genuinely specializing lawyers, manage identity and consent, and secure every user's most sensitive information to the standard the law and their trust demand.

**The Admin Dashboard — the platform's control tower.** The operational surface through which we onboard and verify advocates, curate specializations, monitor answer quality and safety, manage the knowledge corpus, and uphold the standards that make the platform trustworthy.

**Scope, deliberately drawn.** Law is vast; trust is fragile. We will focus, at least at first, on the legal services and disputes that ordinary Indian citizens and small businesses actually need day to day — spanning transactional services (affidavits and notary work, drafting and vetting of agreements, legal notices, incorporation and structuring, digital business onboarding, IP registration and portfolio management, employment and contractor agreements, estate planning, real estate conveyancing, marriage registration, corporate secretarial and MCA compliance, commercial debt recovery, tax advisory, M&A and joint ventures, capital markets, project and infrastructure financing, competition law, artist contract management, powers of attorney, vehicle and land sale deeds, rental agreements, wills, legal heir certificates, RTI applications, and public-official accountability) through to the everyday disputes (traffic and road-safety, property, matrimonial and family, employment and labour, consumer and fair-trade, criminal offences and FIR issues, cybercrime and online fraud, cheque bounce, landlord-tenant and tenant-society, business and partnership, financial, data privacy, public litigation, succession, taxation and customs, insurance, IP, e-commerce, international trade, insolvency, social security, education, health and medical, and the safety and dignity issues — online trolling, cyberbullying, women's safety and privacy — that the new criminal code takes seriously for the first time). We would rather be exceptional and trusted across the legal problems that touch the most lives than mediocre across all of law.

## 5. The Future We Are Building Toward

We measure our success not in downloads but in a changed relationship between Indians and their own legal system.

**A five-year horizon.** We see an India where checking your legal position before you sign, complain, or capitulate is simply a reflex — the way people now check a map before a journey or a review before a purchase. Where "I'll check LawSafe" is the natural first response to "is this even legal?" Where a citizen in a Tier-3 town has the same instant clarity about their rights as a corporate executive in a metro. Where the volume of misery caused by pure _ignorance_ of one's rights has fallen sharply — not because we replaced lawyers, but because we made the first mile of legal understanding free and universal.

**A ten-year horizon.** We aim to be part of the plumbing of Indian justice itself — reducing the flood of avoidable and ill-formed disputes reaching the courts by resolving confusion upstream, channeling suitable matters toward faster mechanisms, and doing our part in the national effort, championed by NITI Aayog's _ODR Policy Plan_ and the judiciary, to make online, technology-first dispute resolution the point of first recourse. The tailwinds are real: the government's eCourts Phase III programme commits ₹7,210 crore to digitising courts through 2027. If our existence measurably lightens the load on the courts and measurably raises how many Indians can actually exercise the rights they already have on paper, we will have succeeded.

**A generational aspiration.** India's global justice-gap context is stark — the World Justice Project's _Measuring the Justice Gap_ (2019) estimated that 5.1 billion people worldwide have at least one unmet justice need, including 1.4 billion who cannot meet everyday civil and administrative justice needs. We intend to prove, from India and for India, that AI done responsibly can bend that curve for a billion-plus people, and to build the template that other large, plural, multilingual democracies can learn from.

## 6. Guiding Principles — Our North Star

**North Star: Trust per interaction.** Not users, not sessions, not revenue — the single metric beneath everything is whether each interaction left a citizen more accurately informed and safely served than before. Growth that erodes trust is not growth; it is debt.

Everything we build is judged against these principles:

1. **Grounded or silent.** Our engine speaks only from current, citable Indian law. We would rather the product say "I need a human lawyer for this" than invent a confident falsehood. This is non-negotiable, because the industry has already shown the alternative is catastrophic: Stanford RegLab's _Large Legal Fictions_ (2024) found general-purpose models (GPT-4, Llama 2, PaLM 2) hallucinate on legal queries **between 58% and 88% of the time**, and its follow-up study found even purpose-built retrieval-augmented tools still fabricate — roughly 17% for Lexis+ AI and 34% for Westlaw's AI-Assisted Research. Every substantive answer we give carries its legal basis so it can be checked. We are building a system whose humility is a feature.
1. **Assistance, never a verdict.** We inform and connect; we do not adjudicate or replace the judgment of a qualified advocate. Our AI is a first-mile guide, and we design every experience to make clear where information ends and professional representation begins — mirroring the cautious, human-in-the-loop posture the Indian judiciary itself has adopted with tools like SUPACE and SUVAS, and Kerala's court AI policy that bars generative AI from drafting judgments or predicting outcomes.
1. **A neutral bridge, never a tout.** We operate strictly within Bar Council of India norms, including Rule 36 of the BCI Rules, which the Madras High Court (in _PN Vignesh v. Bar Council_) reaffirmed applies even to algorithmic client-to-lawyer matchmaking. We do not let advocates advertise, bid, or buy rankings, and we do not commodify the profession. We match on genuine specialization and verified credentials, transparently, as a neutral connector — because the integrity of the profession is a feature of our product, not an obstacle to it.
1. **Vernacular-first, dignity-first.** English is a barrier, not a bridge, for the vast majority of Indians — recall that 98% of internet users consume content in Indic languages. We build for the citizen who speaks Bhojpuri, Tamil, Marathi, or Bengali, and we design so that a first-time, low-literacy user feels respected, not judged. Accessibility is not a feature tier; it is the product.
1. **Privacy is sacred.** People will tell us the most vulnerable facts of their lives. We treat every word as confidential by default, engineer for the letter and spirit of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and its Rules notified in November 2025, and hold ourselves to the standard that a citizen's legal confidences are as protected on our platform as in a lawyer's chamber.
1. **Verified humans at the end of the line.** Every lawyer on the platform is a real, Bar Council-verified advocate. There are no anonymous "legal influencers" — precisely the phenomenon the BCI condemned in March 2025 — and no unqualified advice-givers. When a citizen is handed to a human, that human is real, credentialed, and accountable.
1. **What we will not do.** We will not give definitive legal opinions dressed as certainty; we will not draft or file anything for use in court without a qualified advocate in the loop; we will not sell user data or monetize attention; we will not let commercial pressure override the grounded-or-silent rule. These constraints define us as much as our features do.

## 7. Strategic Differentiation — Why We Win

The opportunity is large and the sector is accelerating. India's legal-services market is estimated by Mordor Intelligence at USD 2.64 billion in 2026, and CIIE.CO (IIM Ahmedabad) has found India to be the world's **second-largest legal-tech ecosystem** behind the USA — a base Tracxn now counts at 1,118 startups (roughly 800 active, 2 unicorns). The legal-AI slice specifically is, per Grand View Research, among the fastest-growing in the Asia-Pacific, projected to grow from USD 29.5 million in 2024 to USD 106.3 million by 2030 at a 23% CAGR. Consumer legal platforms — Vakilsearch, LegalKart, LawRato and others — already exist. So why us, and why now?

**1. Chat-first understanding, not directory-first search.** Incumbent consumer platforms are, at their core, marketplaces: they help you _find and book_ a lawyer, typically starting from a paid consultation (₹199–₹499 per session is the going rate). We invert the funnel. We start by helping the citizen _understand their problem for free_, and only then — if and when it's genuinely needed — connect them to a human. Understanding first, transaction second. That is a fundamentally different, and far more trusted, relationship with the user.

**2. A model built for Indian law, not adapted to it.** Our defensibility is not a chat interface — anyone can wrap an API. It is the domain-grounded engine and the proprietary, growing corpus of Indian statutes, case files, and instruments behind it, tuned specifically to reason within current Indian law and to fail safe. In a field where the whole industry's credibility hinges on not hallucinating, being the platform that Indians trust to be _right_ is the entire game.

**3. Trust as the moat.** Bar Council-verified lawyers, DPDP-grade privacy, cited answers, and a neutral non-solicitation stance are not compliance overhead — they are the brand. The name says it: a citizen should be able to look at LawSafe and know, before reading a word of copy, what we are promising them. In a market where the regulator is actively cracking down on unethical legal advertising and self-styled influencers (the BCI's March 2025 directives and cease-and-desist notices to online portals), being conspicuously, structurally trustworthy is the durable competitive advantage.

**4. Genuine national reach.** Vernacular-first, mobile-first, and designed for the next several hundred million users — not just the English-speaking metro elite that most legal platforms implicitly target. The demographic tailwind (886M+ internet users, 98% consuming Indic-language content) and the policy tailwind (Digital India, eCourts Phase III, the national ODR push) point in the same direction we do.

**5. A two-sided flywheel with real gravity.** Better grounding attracts more citizens; more citizens with well-structured, pre-qualified needs attract more verified specialist lawyers; more lawyers and more resolved matters deepen the corpus and sharpen the matching; which improves grounding again. Each turn makes the platform more accurate, more useful, and harder to replicate.
